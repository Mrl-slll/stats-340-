6. Random Variables in a Coffee Shop (1pt each, 8pts total)

A small coffee shop records the number of customers arriving each morning between 8–9 a.m. They have noticed that:

  • On average, 12 customers arrive in that hour. The arrivals can be modeled as a Poisson random variable.

  • Each customer spends, on average, $5.50 with a standard deviation of $1.50. The spending per customer is independent and approximately follows a Normal distribution.

Let \(X\) be the number of customers who arrive between 8–9 a.m.

  1. Write down the distribution of \(X\).
lambda<-12
lambda
## [1] 12
  1. What is the probability that exactly 10 customers arrive in an hour?
dpois(10,lambda = 12)
## [1] 0.1048373
  1. What is the probability that at least 15 customers arrive in an hour?
1-ppois(14,lambda = 12)
## [1] 0.2279755

Let \(Y\) be the spending of a single customer.

  1. State the distribution of \(Y\).

Y:Y<- N(5.50,1.50^2)

  1. What is the probability a single customer spends more than $6?
1-pnorm(6,mean = 5.50, sd=1.50)
## [1] 0.3694413

Now let \(W\) be the total revenue between 8–9 a.m.

  1. What is the expected value of \(W\)?
exp_val<-12*5.50
exp_val
## [1] 66
  1. What is the variance of \(W\)? (hint: \(W\) is a random sum: \(W=\sum_{i=1}^X Y_i\). The variance of a random sum is \(Var(W)=EX\cdot VarY + VarX \cdot E^2Y\))
EX <- 12
VarX <- 12         
EY <- 5.50
VarY <- 1.50^2     

VarW <- (EX * VarY) + (VarX * (EY^2))
VarW
## [1] 390
  1. Explain why the exact distribution of \(W\) is complicated. Suggest a method that could be used to approximate it.

Usually, probability problems only have one moving part. Here, two independent things fluctuate at the exact same time:

You don’t know how many people walk through the door (it could be 5, 12, or 20).

You don’t know how much money each individual spends (one person buys a 3 dollar drip coffee; another spends $15 on lattes and pastries)

Normal Approximation (Central Limit Theorem)Since the expected number of arrivals (\(\lambda = 12\)) is moderately large, \(W\) can be approximated using a single Normal distribution parameterized by the mean \(E[W]\) and variance \(\text{Var}(W)\) found in parts f and g:\[W \approx \text{Normal}(\mu = 66, \, \sigma^2 = 390)\]
